Visual design · Photoshop

Charles & Keith — campaign study

Trying out a fashion banner with soft colours and layered images.

Individual coursework · Digital Visual Design · Year 2, Semester 1

About this project

This was a Photoshop coursework project inspired by Charles & Keith. I explored different banner ideas before putting together a final fashion composition. It was a student exercise, rather than work commissioned by the brand.

What I worked on

  • Tried different banner ideas before choosing the final layout.
  • Combined photographs, product images and text using masking, blending and colour adjustments.
  • Used a store photo I took, with AI-assisted background editing recorded in my project write-up.

The idea

The assignment was to create a fashion banner that brought product images, photography and text together.

How I put it together

Trying more than one idea

I explored a simpler arrangement with handbags and display blocks, as well as a busier collage with store images, models and products.

Bringing the final banner together

The final version combines a model, handbags, the store background and collection text with a soft, neutral colour palette.

Working with different images

I used my own outlet photograph alongside brand and product images. Photoshop edits and AI-assisted background editing helped bring the separate pieces into the composition.

Visual walkthrough

How it turned out

The project includes a mind map, two early concepts and the final banner. Together, they show how the visual idea developed.

Reflection

This project gave me room to try different ways of arranging the same kind of content. A simpler product layout and a fuller collage create quite different feelings, even when both are about fashion. Comparing those directions gave me practice looking at the overall composition as well as each individual image.

Putting the final banner together also meant paying attention to how photographs, products and text sat beside one another. Colours, spacing and scale all played a part in making the separate elements feel connected. It was a useful exercise in building one image from several pieces, while keeping a clear visual direction.
