A portfolio, finding its structure

Finding a layout that feels like me and gives my work room to breathe.

Individual project · Digital Visual Design · Year 2, Semester 1

About this project

This was my portfolio design for Digital Visual Design. I used cream and sage colours, serif headings and nature-inspired images, and made layouts for both desktop and mobile.

What I worked on

  • Put together a shared look for the desktop and mobile designs.
  • Changed my first draft after my lecturer suggested adding clearer sections.
  • Organised the final design into an introduction, skills, projects and contact information.

The idea

I wanted to design a portfolio that introduced me and gave visitors a clear way to look through my work.

How I put it together

Keeping the look connected

I used the same colours, heading style and nature-inspired images across the main page and project pages.

Giving each part its own space

My first draft focused mainly on the introduction. After feedback, I added separate areas for my skills, projects and contact information.

Making room on a smaller screen

For mobile, I stacked and rearranged the content instead of just making the desktop design smaller.

What changed after feedback

My lecturer pointed out that the first draft needed clearer sections. I took that feedback and split the content into separate parts. You can see the difference between the first draft and final design below.

Visual walkthrough

How it turned out

I finished desktop and mobile designs for the main page and projects page. The portfolio website you are browsing grew from that original design direction.

Reflection

The most useful part of this project was seeing how much the layout changed after one piece of feedback. My first draft gave most of the space to the introduction, but it did not have clear sections for the rest of the content. Adding those sections gave each part a place and helped me think about what someone would actually look for in a portfolio.

Designing both desktop and mobile versions also meant thinking about the same content in different amounts of space. I could keep the colours and overall feeling while changing how the page was arranged. This project is especially personal because it became a starting point for the website that now holds my other work.
